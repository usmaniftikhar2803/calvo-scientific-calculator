About these Financial Ratios formulas

Ratio analysis turns the figures in a balance sheet and income statement into numbers you can compare across years and across companies. The formulas below are grouped by purpose: can the business pay its short-term bills (liquidity), is it earning enough (profitability), how much does it rely on borrowed money (leverage), how quickly does it turn stock and debtors into cash (activity), and how does the market value its shares.

Textbooks sometimes define the same ratio slightly differently, for example ROCE or net debt. If your teacher or exam gives a definition, follow that one.

How to use these formulas

  • Start from the question you are asked to answer, then pick the ratio group. Do not compute every ratio for every problem.
  • For turnover ratios, use the average of opening and closing balances when both are given. Use the closing figure only when the question gives nothing else.
  • A ratio means little alone. Compare it with the previous year, with a competitor, or with an industry average before you comment.

Worked example

Question. A company has current assets of Rs. 600,000, inventory of Rs. 200,000, current liabilities of Rs. 400,000, net sales of Rs. 2,000,000, cost of goods sold of Rs. 1,200,000, net profit of Rs. 200,000, total assets of Rs. 1,500,000 and shareholders' equity of Rs. 900,000.

Current ratio = 600,000 / 400,000 = 1.50

Quick ratio = (600,000 − 200,000) / 400,000 = 1.00

Gross profit ratio = (2,000,000 − 1,200,000) / 2,000,000 × 100 = 40.0%

Net profit ratio = 200,000 / 2,000,000 × 100 = 10.0%

ROE = 200,000 / 900,000 × 100 = 22.2% and ROA = 200,000 / 1,500,000 × 100 = 13.3%

ROE is higher than ROA because the company uses borrowed money as well as equity.

Common mistakes

  • Using total sales where the formula asks for net credit sales, for example in debtors turnover.
  • Mixing the denominators of the return ratios. ROE uses shareholders' equity, ROA uses total assets, and ROCE uses capital employed (total assets minus current liabilities).
  • Assuming a higher ratio is always better. A very high current ratio can mean idle cash or slow-moving stock.

FAQ

What is a good current ratio?

There is no single right number. Many textbooks quote 2:1 for the current ratio and 1:1 for the quick ratio as rules of thumb, but acceptable levels depend on the industry. A retailer that sells stock quickly can work with lower ratios than a manufacturer.

What is the difference between ROE and ROCE?

ROE shows the profit earned for shareholders as a percentage of shareholders' equity. ROCE compares operating profit (EBIT) with all the long-term capital employed, so it shows how well the whole business uses its capital before the effect of financing choices.

How do I calculate the cash conversion cycle?

Add days inventory outstanding and days sales outstanding, then subtract days payables outstanding. A shorter cycle means cash is tied up in operations for less time.
