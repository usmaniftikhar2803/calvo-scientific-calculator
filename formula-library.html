Formula Library

By Hafiz Muhammad Usman · Last updated 1 November 2026 · Search, browse by class, print

The Formula Library collects more than 5,500 formulas and 4,000 concepts across 21 subjects, from 9th class maths and physics up to engineering, commerce and data science. Instead of flipping through a textbook for one equation, type a keyword and open the formula you need.

You can also browse the way a school or college syllabus is arranged. The class-wise view groups formulas into 9th class, 10th class, 1st year (FSc Part-I) and 2nd year (FSc Part-II), and there is an all-classes view with search and print.

What it can do

  • Search by keyword: type a topic, a symbol or a name such as kinetic energy or mole and the matching formulas appear.
  • Browse by class: 9th, 10th, 1st year and 2nd year each have their own pages for physics, chemistry, maths, biology and computer science. See the class-wise formula pages.
  • Understand, not just copy: many formulas open with a short explanation, and a number have an Urdu note and a solved example. The AI explanation can break a formula down further when you add your own free Gemini key.
  • Test yourself: the same formulas power the formula quiz, so you can practise recalling them.
  • Print: the all-classes view can be printed, which is handy for a revision sheet.

How to use it

  1. Open the Formula Library and pick a class level, or choose all classes.
  2. Pick a subject, or use the search box to jump straight to a topic.
  3. Open a formula to see its symbols, units and any explanation or solved example.
  4. Use the quiz to check that you can recall it without looking.

A formula in use: Newton's second law

The law is F = m × a. A 5 kg trolley accelerating at 3 m/s² needs a force of 5 × 3 = 15 N. The library lists the symbols and units (newton, kilogram, metres per second squared) so you can check that your answer has the right unit, not only the right number.

Tips for better results

  • Check the unit of every symbol before you substitute. Mixing km and m is the most common mistake in numerical problems.
  • Formulas are reference material. Your textbook and teacher decide which ones your exam expects.
  • Spotted a wrong or missing formula? Tell the developer through the contact page and it will be corrected.

Frequently asked questions

How many formulas are there?

More than 5,500 formulas, plus over 4,000 concepts and rules, across 21 subjects. The library keeps growing.

Is it organised by Punjab or Federal Board syllabus?

It is organised by class level (9th, 10th, 1st year and 2nd year). Always follow your own board's syllabus for what is examined.

Does it work offline?

Formula data is saved on your device after you have used it, so many pages keep working offline.

Can I get an Urdu explanation?

Selected formulas include a short Urdu note, and the AI solver has an option to explain in Urdu.
