How CGPA is calculated

CGPA = (Σ semester GPA × semester credit hours) ÷ (total credit hours of all semesters).

Example: Semester 1: GPA 3.50 with 18 credit hours = 63.00 points. Semester 2: GPA 3.20 with 18 credit hours = 57.60 points. Total = 120.60 points over 36 credit hours, so CGPA = 120.60 ÷ 36 = 3.35.

Important: universities differ in grading scale and in how failed or repeated courses count, so always confirm with your official transcript. This calculator is for estimating.

Understanding your CGPA: a practical guide for Pakistani students

A CGPA looks like a single number, but it is really a summary of every grade you have earned, weighted by how many credit hours each course carried. Once you see how that weighting works, you can predict your CGPA before results are announced, plan which semester to work hardest in, and spot a mistake on a transcript. This guide walks through the logic behind the calculator above and the places where students most often go wrong.

Why credit hours matter more than the number of semesters

The most common shortcut is to add up each semester's GPA and divide by the number of semesters. That works only when every semester has the same number of credit hours. Take four semesters with these results: 3.10 on 17 credit hours, 3.42 on 18, 2.85 on 18 and 3.67 on 19. The weighted CGPA is (3.10 × 17 + 3.42 × 18 + 2.85 × 18 + 3.67 × 19) ÷ 72 = 235.29 ÷ 72 = 3.27. A plain average of the four GPAs gives 3.26, so here the shortcut is almost right.

It breaks down when the loads are uneven. Imagine a student who took a light summer-style semester of 9 credit hours and scored 3.80, then a heavy regular semester of 21 credit hours and scored 2.40. The weighted CGPA is (3.80 × 9 + 2.40 × 21) ÷ 30 = 2.82, while the plain average says 3.10. That is a gap of almost 0.3, enough to decide whether a scholarship or MS admission condition is met. Universities always use the weighted method, so your estimate must too.

How to use the calculator step by step

What your CGPA means on the HEC scale

Most public and private universities in Pakistan follow the 4.0 scale recommended by the Higher Education Commission. On that table 3.67 to 4.00 is an A (85 marks and above), 3.01 to 3.33 is B+ (75 to 79 marks), 2.01 to 2.33 is C+ (64 to 67 marks) and anything below 1.00 is a fail. A CGPA of 3.00 therefore corresponds roughly to a B, which is the 71 to 74 mark band, and a CGPA of 2.00 is the usual minimum to keep a degree on track. Many universities place a student on academic probation when the CGPA falls below 2.00, and some set a higher floor for scholarships or for Dean's honour lists. Your own university handbook is the final authority on these limits.

Repeated, failed and withdrawn courses

Policies differ here, and this is where two students with similar marks can end up with different CGPAs.

  • Failed course (F): the course earns zero grade points but its credit hours still count in the denominator until you repeat it.
  • Repeat with replacement: some universities delete the old grade from the CGPA once you retake the course and keep only the new one.
  • Repeat with averaging: others count both attempts, so the old low grade keeps pulling the CGPA down.
  • Withdrawn course (W): usually carries no grade points and no credit hours in the CGPA, but check the deadline, because a withdrawal after the cut-off date may be recorded as an F.
  • Non-credit courses: remedial or zero-credit courses should not be added to the calculator at all.

To reproduce a transcript, enter the GPA and credit hours exactly as your university counts them. If your university replaces repeated grades, use the semester GPA after the repeat is applied.

Common mistakes that give a wrong CGPA

  • Averaging semester GPAs without weighting them by credit hours.
  • Typing your cumulative GPA from the previous result card as if it were that semester's GPA, which counts the old semesters twice.
  • Mixing scales, for example one semester on a 4.0 scale and another on a relative or 5.0 scale. Convert them first or ask the examination office.
  • Rounding each semester GPA to one decimal place before entering it. Use the two-decimal value printed on the result card.
  • Counting credit hours of courses that were dropped, or leaving out a course whose result is still pending.
  • Treating a CGPA as a percentage. A 3.20 CGPA is not 32%. Use the CGPA to percentage converter when a form asks for marks.

Using your CGPA to make decisions

The longer you study, the harder it is to move your CGPA, because each new semester is only a share of the total. A student with 100 credit hours already completed who scores a perfect 4.00 in the next 18 credit hours will raise a 3.00 CGPA to only 3.15. That is why the first two years matter so much, and why a bad first semester is easier to repair early than late. If you want a specific number, such as 3.00 for a scholarship renewal or 3.30 for a particular MS programme, the target CGPA calculator tells you the GPA you need next semester and whether the target is still reachable.

For job applications, many Pakistani employers and universities ask for CGPA and the scale together, such as "3.21 / 4.00". Always write the scale. When a form wants a percentage, quote the figure your university prints on its equivalence letter if it issues one, rather than your own conversion.

A quick checklist before you trust the result

Compare the calculator's answer with the cumulative CGPA printed on your latest result card. If the two differ by more than 0.01 or 0.02, one of three things is usually responsible: a repeated course is handled differently, a zero-credit course was included, or one semester's credit hours were entered incorrectly. Fix those and the numbers will normally match. If they still do not, contact your examination office with your result card, because transcript errors do happen and are easier to correct early.

FAQ

How do I calculate CGPA from semester GPAs?

Multiply each semester's GPA by that semester's total credit hours, add these values, then divide by the total credit hours of all semesters.

What is the difference between GPA and CGPA?

GPA is your average for one semester. CGPA is the cumulative average across all completed semesters, weighted by credit hours.

Yes. Use Share result to send it on WhatsApp, Copy link to get a link that reopens your entries, or Print / Save as PDF. Your entries are also remembered on this device.
