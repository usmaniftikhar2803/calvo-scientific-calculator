Percentage formulas

  • X% of Y = (X ÷ 100) × Y
  • X is what % of Y = (X ÷ Y) × 100
  • Percentage change = ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100
  • Increase by P% = Number × (1 + P/100); decrease by P% = Number × (1 − P/100)

Examples

Exam marks: you scored 412 out of 550. Percentage = 412 ÷ 550 × 100 = 74.91%.
Discount: a Rs. 2,500 item with 20% off costs 2500 × 0.8 = Rs. 2,000.
Growth: from 80 to 100 is a change of (20 ÷ 80) × 100 = 25% increase.

Reverse percentages: finding the original price

The most common mistake in shopping maths is adding the discount back to the sale price. If a jacket costs Rs. 1,800 after a 25% discount, the original price is not 1,800 + 25%. The sale price is 75% of the original, so Original = Sale price ÷ (1 − 0.25) = 1,800 ÷ 0.75 = Rs. 2,400. The same idea works for tax: a bill of Rs. 5,900 that includes 18% GST has a base price of 5,900 ÷ 1.18 = Rs. 5,000, so the tax is Rs. 900.

Successive percentages

Percentages do not add up when they are applied one after another, because each step changes the base. A 20% rise followed by a 20% fall is 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96, a net 4% loss. Multiply the factors instead of adding the percentages.

Step 1Step 2Combined factorNet change
+10%+10%1.10 × 1.10 = 1.21+21%
+20%−20%1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96−4%

To find 10% of any number, move the decimal point one place left; 5% is half of that, and 15% is 10% plus 5%. For example, 15% of 840: 10% = 84, 5% = 42, total 126.

Worked example: board exam percentage

A student scores 892 out of 1,100 marks. Percentage = 892 ÷ 1,100 × 100 = 81.09%. To reach 85% the student would need 0.85 × 1,100 = 935 marks, which is 43 marks more. Working backwards from a target percentage like this is useful when planning how many marks to aim for in the remaining papers.

FAQ

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by the number. For example, 15% of 200 = 0.15 × 200 = 30.

How do I find the percentage of marks?

Divide obtained marks by total marks and multiply by 100.

What is the difference between percentage change and percentage points?

Going from 10% to 15% is a change of 5 percentage points, but a 50% relative increase.

Why does a 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease not return to the start?

Because the decrease applies to the larger new number. 100 → 120 → 96.
