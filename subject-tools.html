Subject Tools

By Hafiz Muhammad Usman · Last updated 3 November 2026 · Nine subject groups, about 50 calculators each

Subject Tools is a collection of small, focused calculators grouped by subject. Instead of one general calculator, each tool asks for exactly the inputs a formula needs (with labels and units) and returns the answer.

There are nine groups, each with about 50 tools: Chemistry, Physics, Biology, Commerce, Math, Finance, Vectors, Calculus and Probability.

What it can do

  • Chemistry: molar mass and molarity, pH, an equation balancer, the ideal gas law, dilution (M₁V₁ = M₂V₂), percent composition and yield, gas laws, titration and half-life.
  • Physics: projectile motion, Ohm's law, kinematics, work, energy and power, F = ma, density, pendulum period, wave speed, lens and mirror formula, torque and resistors in series or parallel.
  • Biology: BMI and BMR, Punnett square, Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, population growth, serial dilution and Michaelis-Menten kinetics.
  • Commerce and finance: simple and compound interest, profit and loss, break-even, loan EMI, CAGR, ROI, NPV, IRR, amortization and common financial ratios.
  • Maths, vectors, calculus and probability: permutations and combinations, sequences, dot and cross products, tangent lines, Newton-Raphson, Riemann sums, binomial and Poisson probability and Bayes' theorem.

How to use it

  1. Open Subject Tools and choose a subject group.
  2. Pick a tool from the list. Each has labelled inputs.
  3. Enter your values and press Calculate.
  4. Check units and magnitude of the answer before you use it.

Ohm's law in two lines

A 12 V supply across a 4 Ω resistor gives I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A. Enter V and R in the Ohm's law tool and check that it gives the same current. By hand, the power is P = V × I = 12 × 3 = 36 W.

Tips for better results

  • Use the tool to check your own working, not to replace it, especially in exams where steps are marked.
  • Medical-style tools such as BMI or BMR give general estimates and are not a substitute for advice from a doctor.

Worked examples from each subject group

  • Chemistry: the molar volume of an ideal gas at 273.15 K and 101,325 Pa is V = nRT/P = 8.314 × 273.15 ÷ 101,325 ≈ 0.02241 m³, or 22.41 litres per mole.
  • Physics: a pendulum 1 m long has a period T = 2π√(L/g) = 2π√(1/9.81) ≈ 2.006 s.
  • Biology: BMI = weight in kg ÷ height in m². For 70 kg and 1.75 m, 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9. Under Hardy-Weinberg, if q² = 0.04 then q = 0.2, p = 0.8 and the heterozygote frequency 2pq = 0.32.
  • Commerce: compound interest on Rs. 100,000 at 10% for 3 years gives 100,000 × 1.1³ = Rs. 133,100.
  • Finance: a Rs. 1,000,000 loan at 12% a year over 12 months has a monthly rate of 1%, and an EMI of P r(1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1) ≈ Rs. 88,849.

How to avoid wrong answers

  • Check the units in each box. Many tools expect SI units such as metres and seconds, or a rate written per year or per month.
  • Interest rates: make the rate and the number of periods match. A yearly rate used with months gives an answer that is off by a factor of twelve.
  • Round at the end. Rounding in the middle of a calculation, especially in finance, changes the last digits.
  • Sanity check: compare the result with a rough estimate. A BMI of 229 or a pendulum period of 20 seconds for a 1 m string signals a unit slip.

When a small tool is better than the main calculator

A scientific calculator needs you to remember the formula and enter it correctly. A subject tool names each input and shows the unit, so it is easier to use for a formula you use rarely and easier to check against your textbook method.

Frequently asked questions

How many tools are there?

About 450 across nine subject groups.

Do the tools show steps?

Each tool asks for labelled inputs and returns the result. For step-by-step algebra use the equation solver or the calculus pages.

Does it work offline?

Yes, once loaded on your device.

Do the tools show working steps?

They focus on giving a labelled input form and a clear answer. For step-by-step working, use the derivative, integral, limit and equation tools.

Can I use the results in a lab report?

Yes, as a check on your own calculation. Show your formula and substitution in the report, and quote your answer to a sensible number of significant figures.
