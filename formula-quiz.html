Formula Quiz & Flashcards

By Hafiz Muhammad Usman · Last updated 3 November 2026 · Quiz, practice MCQs and flashcards

Reading a formula and recalling it in an exam are different skills. The quiz section turns the formula library into practice: you choose a subject and a number of questions, answer, and see straight away what you got right.

It has three modes. Formula Quiz asks about formulas from any subject, Practice MCQs uses a separate bank of multiple-choice questions, and Flashcards shows one card at a time so you can flip it and rate how well you knew it.

What it can do

  • Pick your subject: quiz on one subject, or mix all of them.
  • Choose the length: 5, 10, 15 or 20 questions, depending on how much time you have.
  • Practice MCQs with difficulty: filter the practice bank by difficulty.
  • Flashcards with self-rating: flip the card, then mark it Again, Hard, Good or Easy so you know what to repeat.
  • Learn from mistakes: after a quiz, review the questions you got wrong, or try again straight away.

How to use it

  1. Open the quiz and choose Formula Quiz, Practice MCQs or Flashcards.
  2. Pick a subject (or all subjects) and the number of questions.
  3. Answer each question, then read the result and review any wrong answers.
  4. Repeat the weak topics a day later, which works better than one long session.

A simple 10-minute routine

Pick one subject you struggled with, do a 10-question quiz, then open Review Wrong Answers. Look each missed formula up in the formula library, and run the same quiz again the next day. Flashcards are best on a phone while you wait or travel.

Tips for better results

  • Aim to understand where a formula comes from. It is easier to remember than a bare string of symbols.
  • Mixed-subject quizzes feel harder but prepare you better for exam day.

Why testing yourself beats rereading

Rereading a formula page feels productive because everything looks familiar, but familiarity is not the same as being able to recall it under exam pressure. Research on retrieval practice consistently finds that trying to recall an answer, even when you get it wrong, helps memory more than looking at it again. That is the idea behind a quiz and flashcards: you spend your time on the formulas you cannot yet produce yourself.

Know more than the formula

For each formula, learn the meaning of every symbol, its unit and the condition under which it holds. You can often rebuild a formula instead of memorising it. For example, from v = u + at and s = ut + ½at², eliminate t. From the first equation t = (v − u)/a. Substituting gives s = u(v − u)/a + (v − u)²/(2a) = (v² − u²)/(2a), so v² = u² + 2as. If you can derive it once, you are less likely to misremember it.

A simple weekly pattern

  • Day 1: read the chapter's formulas, then take a 10-question quiz in that subject.
  • Day 2: review only the questions you missed, then retake the quiz.
  • Day 4 and Day 7: a short mixed quiz that includes older chapters.
  • The night before a paper: flashcards on the “Again” and “Hard” cards only.

Using wrong answers well

A wrong answer is useful information. Write the correct formula in your notebook, note why you chose the other option (mixed-up variable, wrong unit, wrong condition), and look up the topic in the class-wise formula pages. Short sessions of 10 to 15 minutes spread over several days work better than one long session the evening before.

Frequently asked questions

Are the questions from my board's paper?

No. They are practice questions and formula recall, not past papers. Use them alongside your own syllabus and past papers.

Do I need an account?

No. Everything runs in your browser.

Does it work offline?

Once the data has been loaded on your device, quizzes keep working offline.

Should I memorise formulas or understand them?

Both. Understanding helps you rebuild a forgotten formula, and recall under time pressure needs practice. Use the quiz for recall and the formula pages for understanding.

How many questions should I do in one sitting?

Ten to fifteen is enough for most people. Stop when you start guessing, and come back the next day for the questions you missed.
