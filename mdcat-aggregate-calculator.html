The formula

MDCAT merit is a weighted percentage: Aggregate = (Matric ÷ Matric total × 10) + (FSc ÷ FSc total × 40) + (MDCAT ÷ 180 × 50). The MDCAT is marked out of 180 MCQs, one mark each, with no negative marking.

PartWeightageMarks used
Matric (SSC)10%Total marks, e.g. 1100
FSc (HSSC)40%Full FSc, e.g. 1100
MDCAT50%Score out of 180

Worked example

Matric 950 of 1100 at 10%: 950 ÷ 1100 × 10 = 8.64. FSc 980 of 1100 at 40%: 980 ÷ 1100 × 40 = 35.64. MDCAT 150 of 180 at 50%: 150 ÷ 180 × 50 = 41.67. Aggregate = 8.64 + 35.64 + 41.67 = 85.94%.

What about NUMS?

NUMS (Army medical colleges) has its own test and uses a different split: FSc 50% + NUMS test 50% with no Matric. Pick “NUMS” in the formula list above to use it.

Important

The 10 / 40 / 50 split is the PMDC formula for MDCAT-based MBBS and BDS admission. This calculator gives an estimate. Weightages, and even which marks count, can change from one admission cycle to the next, so always confirm with the official prospectus or admission notice. The published merit list is final. MDCAT merit explained: how the aggregate really works

For MBBS and BDS admission in Pakistan, the merit list is not decided by your MDCAT score alone. It is a weighted combination of three results: your Matric (SSC), your FSc (HSSC) and your MDCAT. Knowing exactly how much each part is worth lets you estimate your position, decide how much to push in the last weeks of preparation, and avoid arithmetic errors when you check a published merit list. This guide goes beyond the formula and shows how the numbers behave.

The three parts and what each one is worth

The commonly used formula for public and private medical and dental colleges is 10% Matric, 40% FSc and 50% MDCAT, with the MDCAT marked out of 180. Each result is first turned into a fraction of its total, then multiplied by its weight. Admission authorities such as the University of Health Sciences publish the method in their prospectus, and the weightages have been consistent in recent cycles, but you should still confirm them in the notice for your year and province.

A full worked example

Take a student with 950 of 1100 in Matric, 980 of 1100 in FSc and 150 of 180 in MDCAT.

  • Matric: 950 ÷ 1100 × 10 = 8.64
  • FSc: 980 ÷ 1100 × 40 = 35.64
  • MDCAT: 150 ÷ 180 × 50 = 41.67

Aggregate = 8.64 + 35.64 + 41.67 = 85.94%. Now consider a second student with 880 in Matric, 905 in FSc and 140 in MDCAT. Their aggregate is 8.00 + 32.91 + 38.89 = 79.80%. The first student leads by about six points (85.94 against 79.80). Of that 6.14-point gap, 2.78 points come from the ten-mark difference in MDCAT, 2.73 from FSc and 0.64 from Matric.

Which marks are worth the most?

Weights are applied to percentages, so what matters is how many aggregate points each extra mark is worth.

Extra marksAggregate points gained
1 extra MDCAT mark (out of 180)0.278
10 extra MDCAT marks2.78
10 extra FSc marks (out of 1100)0.36
10 extra Matric marks (out of 1100)0.09

One MDCAT mark is worth almost eight times as much as one FSc mark. That is why MDCAT preparation is the one lever still in your hands after results of Matric and FSc are fixed. It is also why a small number of questions can change your position on a closely packed merit list.

Using the reverse calculator to set a realistic target

Instead of guessing, work backwards. With Matric 880 and FSc 905, a target aggregate of 80% needs about 141 MDCAT marks, 85% needs about 159, and 90% needs about 177. Totals vary: enter what is printed on your certificate

The tool assumes 1100 marks for Matric and FSc because that is the common total, but not every board and group has the same totals. Some certificates show 1050, 900 or another figure, and the University of Health Sciences itself uses examples with totals such as 1050 and 900 in its published merit formula. Always type the total from your own certificate. For example, a Matric score of 950 gives 8.64 points if the total is 1100 and 9.05 points if the total is 1050, which is a difference of 0.41.

Common mistakes

  • Using Part I of FSc instead of the full FSc. For MDCAT merit, the full HSSC (Part I plus Part II) is normally used. Check your notice, because the calculator needs the marks that your admission authority counts.
  • Entering MDCAT out of the wrong total. MDCAT is out of 180. A total of 200 or 100 will inflate or deflate the result.
  • Forgetting bonus marks. Some categories, such as Hafiz-e-Quran, may add marks according to the rules of the admission authority. Use the bonus box only for what your notice allows.
  • Mixing O/A levels and boards. If you studied under a foreign system, you need an equivalence certificate from the Inter Board Committee of Chairmen, and the equivalent marks are what count.
  • Treating the estimate as the final merit. Published merit lists may use rounding rules, and the official list is final.

What the aggregate does not tell you

An aggregate does not show closing merit. That number changes each year with the difficulty of the paper, the number of seats and the number of applicants, and it differs by college and category such as open merit or self-finance. Use your aggregate to compare yourself with last year's closing merit lists, but expect a margin of several points either way. Also remember that provincial domicile, quota seats and admissions through different authorities may apply additional rules.

Planning your remaining preparation

If your Matric and FSc are fixed, divide your target gap by 0.278 to see how many extra MDCAT marks you need. If you want to move from 79.8% to 85%, you need roughly 19 more marks, which is about one extra mark in every ten questions you currently miss. That is a realistic target for focused practice on weak chapters. A student who is already at 160 and wants 170 should look at accuracy and speed rather than new topics.

FAQ

What is the MDCAT aggregate formula?

Aggregate = 10% Matric + 40% FSc + 50% MDCAT. Convert each result to a percentage of its total, multiply by its weightage and add the three parts.

How many marks is the MDCAT out of?

The MDCAT is out of 180 (180 MCQs, one mark each). Enter 180 as the total in the MDCAT row.

Is the MDCAT formula the same in every province?

The PMDC formula of 10/40/50 is used for public sector medical and dental colleges. Some institutions, like NUMS, use their own formula. Check your admission notice.

How many MDCAT marks do I need for a target merit?

Fill in your Matric and FSc marks, type your target aggregate in the second box and press the button. Calvo works out the MDCAT score needed.

Do Hafiz-e-Quran or other bonus marks count?

Some admission policies add bonus marks directly to the aggregate. Enter them in the Bonus box only if your admission notice allows them.

Is the result my official merit?

No. It is an estimate. The merit list published by the admitting authority is final.
