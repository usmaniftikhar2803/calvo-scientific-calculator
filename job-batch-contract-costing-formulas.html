About these Job, Batch & Contract Costing formulas

Job and batch costing are used when work is done to order, or in groups of identical units. Contract costing is the same idea for large projects that run across more than one accounting period, so profit has to be recognised in stages.

The contract formulas follow the traditional rule taught in cost accounting courses: take no profit on a contract that is less than a quarter complete, take a fraction of the notional profit as it progresses, and use the estimated total profit when it is nearly finished. Confirm which rule your syllabus uses.

How to use these formulas

  • For jobs, add direct material, direct labour, direct expenses and absorbed overhead, then apply the markup or margin the question specifies.
  • For contracts, work out work certified, cash received (work certified less retention) and the notional profit before you apply any fraction.
  • Check the completion stage (work certified divided by contract price) to decide which profit formula applies.

Worked example

Question. A contract has a price of Rs. 5,000,000. Work certified to date is Rs. 2,000,000, the retention is 10% and the cost of work to date is Rs. 1,700,000.

Retention = 2,000,000 × 10% = Rs. 200,000, so cash received = 2,000,000 − 200,000 = Rs. 1,800,000.

Stage of completion = 2,000,000 / 5,000,000 = 40%, which is between 25% and 50%.

Notional profit = work certified − cost of work to date = 2,000,000 − 1,700,000 = Rs. 300,000.

Profit credited = ⅓ × 300,000 × (1,800,000 / 2,000,000) = Rs. 90,000.

Common mistakes

  • Using the contract price instead of work certified when you compute the stage of completion or the cash fraction.
  • Forgetting that cash received is work certified minus retention, not the amount billed.
  • Using EBQ without separating the setup cost per batch from the carrying cost per unit.

FAQ

What is retention money in contract costing?

A percentage of each certified amount that the customer holds back until the contract is complete or defects are corrected. It reduces the cash received but not the work certified.

What is the difference between job costing and batch costing?

Job costing tracks the cost of one customer's order. Batch costing tracks the cost of a group of identical units made together and divides it by the batch size to get a cost per unit.

Why is only part of the profit taken on an incomplete contract?

Because of prudence: the final outcome is uncertain, so only a portion of the profit is recognised until the contract is far enough along for the estimate to be reliable.
