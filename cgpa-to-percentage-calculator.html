How the conversion works

HEC does not publish a single formula to turn CGPA into a percentage. It publishes a table that maps marks ranges to grades and grade points. This calculator uses that table: it finds the grade for your CGPA, shows the marks range for that grade, and estimates where you sit inside that range.

Example: a CGPA of 3.20 falls in grade B+ (grade points 3.01 to 3.33), which is 75 to 79 marks. Placing 3.20 within that band gives an estimate of about 77.4%.

The rough formula (CGPA ÷ 4 × 100)

Some students and employers use CGPA ÷ 4 × 100. For 3.20 that gives 80%. This is a quick approximation, not an HEC rule, and it usually gives a higher number than the HEC table. Whichever value you need, ask your university or the organisation asking for it which method they accept, because an official equivalence letter from your university is what counts.

Converting CGPA to percentage: what is official, what is an estimate

Every year thousands of Pakistani graduates face the same form field: "Enter your percentage." Their transcript shows a CGPA, and there is no single national formula that turns one into the other. This guide explains the methods people use, why the answers differ, and how to choose the right number for your situation.

Method 1: the HEC grade table

HEC publishes a table that links marks to grades and grade points. A B+ is 75 to 79 marks and 3.01 to 3.33 grade points. A CGPA of 3.20 therefore lies inside the B+ band, and the calculator places it at the corresponding position inside the 75 to 79 range, giving about 77.4%. This is the closest thing to an official conversion, but it is still an estimate because a CGPA is an average of many grades, not a single grade.

Method 2: the rough formula

Many students use CGPA ÷ 4 × 100 or, equivalently, CGPA × 25. For 3.20 that gives 80%. The shortcut ignores the real shape of the grading table, where a B+ sits at 75 to 79 marks, not at 80. For high CGPAs the two methods drift apart: CGPA 3.00 gives 75% by the formula but sits in the 71 to 74 band on the table. For low CGPAs the formula can understate marks. No regulation requires the formula, so use it only when a recruiter or form specifically asks for it.

Method 3: your university's own conversion

Some universities publish an official equivalence statement, for example "CGPA 3.0 equals 70% to 74%", and many will issue an equivalence or conversion letter on request. Where such a letter exists, it overrides both methods above. Ask the examination office or the controller of examinations, because the letter costs little and avoids disputes later.

Which number should you use?

  • Public sector jobs and Federal or provincial commissions: they normally ask for the marks or grade as printed on the degree and transcript. If they need a percentage, use the university's conversion letter.
  • Admission to MS or MPhil in Pakistan: most universities accept CGPA directly, often with a minimum such as 2.50 or 3.00 on the 4.0 scale. Do not convert unless the form forces you to.
  • Foreign universities and credential evaluators: they have their own conversion rules and often ask for official transcripts instead of a self-calculated number.
  • Private sector CVs: write the CGPA with the scale, for example "3.45/4.00". If a percentage field is compulsory, state the method in brackets.

From percentage to CGPA

The reverse conversion is useful for students who studied under the annual system (BA, BSc, ADP) and need to fill a CGPA field. Enter your marks percentage and the calculator returns the grade, the HEC grade point range, and the top value of that range, which is what most transcripts print. For example, 78% falls in B+, which has grade points up to 3.33. If your marks are at the bottom of a band, such as 75%, the true grade point may be lower than the top value, so treat the result as the upper estimate.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Treating CGPA as a percentage. A 3.50 is not 35% or 3.5%. It is 3.50 out of 4.00.
  • Mixing scales. Some universities use 5.0 or 10.0 scales. This tool is for the 4.0 scale, so a 5.0 CGPA needs a different conversion.
  • Using the formula for a transcript line. Never print a formula-based percentage on a document that looks official.
  • Rounding up. If the result is 77.4, write 77.4 or 77, not 78 or 80.
  • Ignoring relative grading. At universities that grade on a curve, marks bands vary by course, so any conversion is only a rough guide.

When the conversion matters most

The conversion usually appears in three places: eligibility cut-offs for jobs, scholarship applications that list a minimum percentage, and admissions abroad that ask for an equivalent. In each case, the safest route is to ask the awarding body which method it accepts, attach your university's equivalence letter if available, and keep this page's estimate as a private check. Using a conservative number protects you if your documents are later verified.

Worked examples for typical situations

A fresh graduate applying to a bank trainee programme. The advertisement asks for "minimum 60% marks or 2.50 CGPA". Fatima has 2.66. Her CGPA meets the second condition directly, so she should write 2.66/4.00 and not convert at all. Converting adds a number that someone may later question.

A student applying for a provincial scholarship that lists "at least 75% marks". Bilal has 3.00 CGPA. The formula gives 75%, but the HEC table places 3.00 in the 71 to 74 band, so he may fall just below the cut-off. He should ask his university for an equivalence letter, which may state a figure that clears the limit or confirms he does not qualify.

A transfer applicant asked for percentage in a form field. Sana has 3.45 CGPA, which sits in the A- band (3.34 to 3.66, or 80 to 84 marks), a little above the bottom of it. She can write "approx. 81%" with the note "converted using HEC grading table" in the remarks field, which is honest and verifiable.

Questions people ask before submitting

Can two universities with the same CGPA have different percentages? Yes. If one uses absolute grading and another uses relative grading, the same CGPA may reflect very different marks. That is the main reason employers prefer official documents to self-calculated numbers.

Is the conversion reversible? Not exactly. A CGPA is an average of grade points, and converting to percentage and back loses information. Treat both directions as estimates and keep your transcript as the source of truth.

What if my university has no equivalence letter? Request a statement of grading scheme from the examination office. Even an official copy of the grade table helps a recruiter or admissions officer interpret your CGPA.

FAQ

How do I convert CGPA to percentage?

Find your grade from the HEC table and read the marks range for it, or use the rough formula CGPA ÷ 4 × 100. The table method follows HEC; the formula is only an approximation.

Is CGPA × 25 the same as percentage?

It is the same as CGPA ÷ 4 × 100. It is a common shortcut but not an official HEC conversion.

Does every university use the same conversion?

No. Universities can use their own scale or relative grading. Ask your university for its official conversion or an equivalence letter.

What CGPA is 60 percent?

Under the HEC table, 58 to 60 marks is grade C- and 61 to 63 is grade C, so 60% is C- with grade points up to 1.66.
