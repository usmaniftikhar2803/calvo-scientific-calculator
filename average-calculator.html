Formulas

  • Mean (average) = sum of all numbers ÷ how many numbers there are.
  • Median = the middle value when the numbers are sorted. With an even count, it is the average of the two middle values.
  • Mode = the value that appears most often (there can be more than one, or none).
  • Range = largest value − smallest value.

Example

Numbers: 12, 15, 15, 20, 28. Sum = 90, count = 5, so mean = 18. Sorted middle value (median) = 15. The value 15 appears twice, so mode = 15. Range = 28 − 12 = 16.

Mean, median and mode: which one to use

The mean is best when values are fairly even and you want a figure that uses every number, such as your average marks across tests. The median is better when a few extreme values would distort the mean, such as salaries or house prices. The mode is useful for the most common value, such as the most popular shoe size. The range tells you how spread out the data is.

Worked example: even count of values

Numbers: 4, 8, 15, 16, 23, 42. The sum is 108 and there are 6 values, so the mean is 108 ÷ 6 = 18. With an even count, the median is the average of the two middle values: (15 + 16) ÷ 2 = 15.5. All values are different, so there is no mode. The range is 42 − 4 = 38.

Worked example: why the median matters

Five monthly salaries in thousands: 20, 22, 24, 25, 150. The mean is 241 ÷ 5 = 48.2, which is higher than four of the five salaries because of the one large value. The median is 24, which describes a typical salary much better.

More than one mode, or none

For 2, 3, 3, 5, 5, 9 the values 3 and 5 both appear twice, so there are two modes: 3 and 5. For 1, 2, 3, 4 no value repeats, and the calculator says there is no mode.

Average marks and what you need next

Test marks 72, 65, 80, 91 and 58 add up to 366, so the average is 366 ÷ 5 = 73.2. If you want an average of 75 after a sixth test, you need a total of 75 × 6 = 450. That means you need 450 − 366 = 84 marks in the sixth test. The same method works for any missing value: required total minus the total so far.

What this tool does not compute

This calculator gives the simple (arithmetic) mean, where every value counts equally. Two other kinds of average come up often in exams.

Weighted average. If assignments are 20% of the grade (score 80), the midterm is 30% (score 70) and the final is 50% (score 90), the result is 0.2 × 80 + 0.3 × 70 + 0.5 × 90 = 16 + 21 + 45 = 82. Do not just average 80, 70 and 90.

Average speed. If you travel to a town at 60 km/h and come back at 40 km/h over the same distance, the average speed is not 50 km/h. It is 2 × 60 × 40 ÷ (60 + 40) = 48 km/h, because you spend more time at the slower speed.

Do not average averages

Class A has 10 students with an average of 60 and class B has 30 students with an average of 80. The combined average is (10 × 60 + 30 × 80) ÷ 40 = 3000 ÷ 40 = 75, not (60 + 80) ÷ 2 = 70. Always combine the totals, not the averages.

FAQ

How do I calculate the average of numbers?

Add all the numbers and divide by how many there are.

What is the difference between mean and median?

The mean uses every value, so extreme values pull it. The median is the middle value and is less affected by extremes.

Can there be more than one mode?

Yes. If several values appear equally often, all of them are modes. If every value is different there is no mode.

Can I paste numbers from a spreadsheet?

Yes. Numbers separated by spaces, commas or new lines all work.

Is "average" the same as "mean"?

In everyday use, yes. Average usually means the arithmetic mean. In statistics, mean, median and mode are all measures of average.

Why is my mean higher than my median?

A few large values are pulling the mean up. This is common with incomes and prices.

How do I find a missing number if I know the average?

Multiply the average by how many numbers there are to get the required total, then subtract the sum of the numbers you already know.

Does the calculator handle weighted averages?

No, it treats every number equally. For weighted results multiply each value by its weight and add the results, as in the example above.
