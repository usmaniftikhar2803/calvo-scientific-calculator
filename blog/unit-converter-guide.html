Unit Converter Guide

August 2026 · 4 min read

Whether you're following an international recipe, working on a physics problem, or just trying to understand a weather report from another country, unit conversion comes up more often than you'd expect. Here's a practical rundown of how the most common conversions work.

Metric vs Imperial

Most of the world uses the metric system (meters, kilograms, Celsius), while the United States primarily uses the imperial system (feet, pounds, Fahrenheit). Converting between the two requires a conversion factor specific to each unit type — there's no single formula that works for all of them.

Length Conversions

1 inch = 2.54 centimeters, and 1 mile = 1.609 kilometers. For quick estimates, remember that a meter is roughly the length of a large stride, and a kilometer is a little over half a mile.

Weight and Mass Conversions

1 kilogram = 2.205 pounds, and 1 ounce = 28.35 grams. Note the technical distinction between weight (a force) and mass (an amount of matter) — in everyday conversions, though, these terms are used interchangeably.

Temperature Conversions

Temperature conversion isn't a simple multiplication — it involves both scaling and an offset:

  • Celsius to Fahrenheit: (°C × 9/5) + 32
  • Fahrenheit to Celsius: (°F − 32) × 5/9

This is why temperature conversions trip people up more often than length or weight — you can't just multiply by a fixed factor.

Volume Conversions

1 liter = 1.057 US quarts, and 1 US gallon = 3.785 liters. Cooking recipes from different countries often mix these units, which is one of the most common real-world reasons people reach for a converter.
