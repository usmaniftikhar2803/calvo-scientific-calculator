GPA vs CGPA — What's the Difference?

August 2026 · 4 min read

Students often use "GPA" and "CGPA" interchangeably, but they measure slightly different things. Knowing the distinction matters when you're applying for scholarships, transferring credits, or simply trying to track your own academic progress.

What Is GPA?

GPA, or Grade Point Average, measures your academic performance over a single term — typically one semester. It's calculated by converting each course grade into a point value (for example, A = 4.0, B = 3.0), multiplying by the credit hours of that course, and dividing the total by the number of credit hours attempted that term.

What Is CGPA?

CGPA, or Cumulative Grade Point Average, measures your academic performance across all semesters or terms completed so far — essentially, it's the running average of every GPA you've earned since you started your program. This is the number most universities look at when evaluating overall academic standing for graduation, honors, or further studies.

How They're Calculated

  1. Convert each course grade to grade points using your institution's scale.
  2. Multiply each grade point by that course's credit hours.
  3. Sum the results and divide by the total credit hours for that period (semester for GPA, entire program so far for CGPA).

Because CGPA is cumulative, one weak semester has less impact on it than it would on that semester's individual GPA — the effect gets diluted as more semesters are added.

Why the Distinction Matters

Scholarship committees, employers, and graduate programs often ask specifically for CGPA because it reflects consistency over time rather than a single term's performance. Knowing both numbers helps you understand not just how you did recently, but how you're trending overall.

A Worked Example

Suppose in Semester 1 you take three courses: a 3-credit course with grade A (4.0), a 4-credit course with grade B (3.0), and a 3-credit course with grade A- (3.7). Your GPA for that semester is: (3×4.0 + 4×3.0 + 3×3.7) / (3+4+3) = (12 + 12 + 11.1) / 10 = 35.1/10 = 3.51. If Semester 2 gives you a GPA of 3.2 across 10 credit hours, your CGPA becomes the weighted average of both semesters combined: (3.51×10 + 3.2×10) / 20 = 3.355. Notice how the CGPA sits between the two semester GPAs — this is exactly why a single bad semester pulls the CGPA down less dramatically than it affects that semester's own GPA.

Grading Scales Vary by Institution

Not every university uses the same scale. Pakistani universities under HEC guidelines commonly use a 4.0 scale where A+ = 4.0, A = 4.0, B+ = 3.4, and so on, while some UET-affiliated programs use slightly different cutoffs. Always check your specific institution's official grading table before calculating manually, since assuming a generic scale can give a misleading result.

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Averaging GPAs instead of weighting by credit hours. If two semesters have a different number of credit hours, simply averaging the two GPA numbers (rather than weighting by credits) gives a slightly inaccurate CGPA.
  • Using the wrong grade-point scale. A grade of "B" might be worth 3.0 on one scale and 3.3 on another — always confirm your institution's specific conversion table.
  • Forgetting to include failed or repeated courses correctly. Depending on institutional policy, a failed and later repeated course may or may not count twice toward CGPA — this varies enough that it's worth confirming with your academic office.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my CGPA be higher than my most recent GPA?
Yes — if your earlier semesters had higher GPAs than your most recent one, the cumulative average can sit above your latest term's individual GPA, since it's blending several semesters together.

Does CGPA reset each year?
No, unlike GPA (which typically applies to a single term), CGPA accumulates across your entire academic program until graduation, unless your institution has a specific policy stating otherwise.
