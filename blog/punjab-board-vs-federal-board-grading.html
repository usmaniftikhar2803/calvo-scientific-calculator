Punjab Board vs Federal Board — Grading Compared

August 2026 · 6 min read

Students moving between cities, or comparing their results with friends on a different board, often run into a simple but confusing question: does a "B" grade mean the same thing on every board? The short answer is mostly yes, but the details are worth understanding.

How Grading Works on Both Boards

Both the Punjab boards (BISE Lahore, Gujranwala, Multan, and others) and the Federal Board (FBISE) follow the same broad structure set by the Inter Board Committee of Chairmen (IBCC): marks are converted into letter grades (A+, A, B, C, D, E) based on percentage ranges, and these ranges are largely standardized nationally so that results are comparable across boards.

Typical Grade Boundaries

  • A+: 90% and above
  • A: 80% – 89%
  • B: 70% – 79%
  • C: 60% – 69%
  • D: 50% – 59%
  • E: 40% – 49% (pass, in some systems)

Exact boundaries can shift slightly year to year based on IBCC policy updates, so always check the current year's official notification rather than relying on older figures.

Where the Boards Genuinely Differ

The core marks-to-grade conversion is standardized, but a few practical differences do exist:

  • Paper pattern and difficulty — question style, the mix of subjective/objective questions, and marking strictness can vary slightly board to board, even on the same syllabus.
  • Result announcement timing — Federal Board and different Punjab boards often announce results on different dates, even for the same academic year.
  • Syllabus scope — while both largely follow the national curriculum, some topic ordering or optional-chapter coverage can differ.

Does It Matter for University Admission?

For most university admissions in Pakistan, what matters is your final percentage (and sometimes your aggregate/merit score), not which specific board issued your certificate — as long as it's a recognized board under IBCC. If you're applying with an aggregate that mixes Matric and FSc percentages from different boards, that's normal and handled the same way regardless of board.
