What to Do After FSc Pre-Medical

August 2026 · 7 min read

Finishing FSc Pre-Medical narrows your options in a useful way, but it doesn't mean MBBS is the only path forward. Here's a realistic look at what comes next, including backup options worth planning for early.

Option 1: MBBS / BDS (Medicine & Dentistry)

The most common path is attempting the MDCAT entry test for admission into MBBS (5 years) or BDS (4 years) programs at public or private medical colleges. Admission is based on a merit formula combining your Matric percentage, FSc percentage, and MDCAT score — the exact weightage varies slightly by province, so check your provincial admission policy for the current year's formula.

Option 2: Allied Health Sciences

If MBBS/BDS doesn't work out on the first attempt, allied health fields are a genuinely strong alternative, not just a fallback: Doctor of Physical Therapy (DPT), Pharm-D, BS Nursing, BS Medical Lab Technology, BS Radiology, and BS Nutrition & Dietetics all build directly on a Pre-Medical background and lead to solid, in-demand careers.

Option 3: Pharm-D (Doctor of Pharmacy)

Pharm-D is a 5-year professional degree that's become increasingly popular, with growing demand in hospital pharmacy, the pharmaceutical industry, and regulatory affairs. Admission is typically based on your FSc percentage (or an entry test, depending on the university), without requiring MDCAT.

Option 4: Non-Medical Fields (Yes, This Is Possible)

A Pre-Medical background doesn't lock you out of non-medical degrees. With an additional deficiency exam or bridging course (subject to the specific university's policy), Pre-Medical students can move into fields like BS Psychology, BS Biotechnology, BS Environmental Science, BS Computer Science (at universities that accept Pre-Medical with a math deficiency course), or business programs (BBA), which typically don't require a science background at all.

How to Decide

  • Apply to MDCAT and allied health programs in parallel — don't wait for MDCAT results before submitting other applications, since deadlines often overlap.
  • Research the actual job market and salary range for allied health fields in your target city, not just the popularity of the field.
  • Talk to people currently working in your shortlisted fields, not just students — day-to-day reality often differs from what a prospectus describes.
