Career Options After Matric

By Hafiz Muhammad Usman · Published August 2026 · Last updated 3 November 2026 · 9 min read

Your Matric stream choice is really a choice about what you will be tested on two years from now. MDCAT, NUST's NET and the ECAT all test FSc-level content, and each one counts your Matric marks toward your final merit. This guide lays out what each path leads to, the merit formulas that apply, and the entry rules that are easy to miss.

Quick comparison

  • FSc Pre-Medical (2 years): MBBS, BDS, Pharm-D, DPT, nursing, MLT. Gate: MDCAT.
  • FSc Pre-Engineering (2 years): BSc Engineering, BS Computer Science, architecture. Gates: NET, ECAT or each university's own test.
  • ICS (2 years): BS Computer Science, Software Engineering, IT. Some engineering programs also accept ICS, so check each university's eligibility table.
  • I.Com (2 years): BBA, BS Accounting & Finance, ACCA, CA (ICAP).
  • DAE (3 years): skilled technical work, or university admission (see the DAE section below).

1. FSc Pre-Medical and the MDCAT

MBBS and BDS admission in Pakistan runs through the MDCAT set by PMDC. It is a paper-based test of 180 MCQs in 3 hours across Biology, Chemistry, Physics, English and Logical Reasoning, and Biology carries the largest share (81 of the 180 questions). There is no negative marking. The qualifying mark for MBBS is 55%, which is 99 correct out of 180.

Final merit is 10% Matric + 40% FSc + 50% MDCAT. Here is a worked example for a student with 1020/1200 in Matric, 950/1100 in FSc and 150/180 in MDCAT:

  • Matric: 1020/1200 = 85.00%, which contributes 8.50
  • FSc: 950/1100 = 86.36%, which contributes 34.55
  • MDCAT: 150/180 = 83.33%, which contributes 41.67
  • Aggregate: 84.71%

Because Matric is 10% of every medical merit list, a weak Matric result costs you in each application. You can check your own numbers with the Aggregate Calculator. For what comes after the degree, see What to Do After FSc Pre-Medical.

2. FSc Pre-Engineering and the NET

NUST builds merit as 75% NET + 15% FSc Part 1 + 10% Matric, and the NET is a 200-mark test. NUST also requires at least 60% in both Matric and FSc before you can apply. A worked example with 1020/1200 in Matric, 470/550 in FSc Part 1 and 140/200 in the NET:

  • Matric: 85.00%, which contributes 8.50
  • FSc Part 1: 85.45%, which contributes 12.82
  • NET: 140/200 = 70%, which contributes 52.50
  • Aggregate: 73.82%

The same student with a NET score of 160/200 would reach about 81.32%. The test moves the result far more than your school marks do. Other universities use different weights. UET Lahore's ECAT, for example, combines the entry test with a weighted academic record, and you can estimate it with the ECAT Aggregate Calculator. Always check the current prospectus for the year you apply. See also What to Do After FSc Pre-Engineering.

3. ICS (Intermediate in Computer Science)

ICS combines computer science, mathematics and physics. It leads directly to BS Computer Science, Software Engineering and IT degrees. Eligibility tables of some public universities list ICS alongside Pre-Engineering for engineering programs, with a minimum aggregate such as 60% plus an entry test, but this varies by university and program. Check the eligibility table of every program you are considering before you commit to ICS, particularly if you want to keep an engineering option open.

4. I.Com and the professional accounting routes

I.Com leads to BBA, BS Accounting & Finance, and the professional qualifications ACCA and CA. Both are open after intermediate:

  • ACCA: open to intermediate (FSc, FA, I.Com, ICS) and A-Level holders. Intermediate students get no exemptions, so you start from the first paper.
  • CA (ICAP): the full-time scheme requires at least 60% in intermediate. Entry also involves a Qualifying Assessment Test covering English, Mathematics and analytical reasoning, followed by Pre-Requisite Competencies where required.
  • BS Accounting & Finance degrees: many are recognized by ICAP and ACCA and give exemptions. Admission rules differ, and some programs expect Maths or Accounting at intermediate level.

5. DAE (Diploma of Associate Engineer)

DAE is a three-year diploma offered through the technical boards (PBTE in Punjab) and run in colleges such as the TEVTA government colleges of technology. Entry is usually via Matric Science, and the merit list for the first year is typically built from your Physics, Chemistry, English and Mathematics marks. Programs include Electrical, Mechanical and Civil.

The old claim that DAE only leads to second-year entry through a bridging exam is out of date. DAE has been declared equivalent to FSc Pre-Engineering for admission purposes, so DAE holders can apply for engineering, technology and computing degrees. Rules still differ by university: some public universities in Punjab accept the relevant DAE with a minimum aggregate such as 60% plus an entry test, and BSc Engineering Technology programs have their own eligibility tables. Confirm the exact rule with the university you are aiming at.

How a stream choice limits your options later

  • Pre-Engineering can lead into computer science and business degrees without extra steps in most cases.
  • Pre-Medical students who want engineering or computer science usually need to clear an additional Mathematics deficiency course, because Pre-Medical has no extra Math paper by default.
  • Pre-Engineering students who want MBBS face the reverse problem: MDCAT tests Biology heavily, and the Biology deficiency exam adds a step.

Your Matric result carries forward whichever route you take, so it is worth calculating it accurately. The Percentage Calculator helps, and Punjab Board vs Federal Board grading explains how marks differ across boards.

A practical way to decide

  1. List 3 or 4 careers that interest you.
  2. For each, check the entry test and eligibility rule on the official site (the sources below).
  3. Choose the stream that is the standard route for most of your list, and prefer the one that keeps a second option open.
  4. Talk to one person currently studying or working in your top choice before you decide.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing Pre-Medical for status when you dislike Biology. MDCAT puts 81 of 180 questions on it.
  • Ignoring Matric marks. They count 10% in both the MDCAT and NUST formulas.
  • Assuming ICS, I.Com or DAE are "lesser" routes. Each is the direct route for a student whose target is already clear.
  • Relying on last year's rules. Test dates, weightages and eligibility change, so check the current prospectus.

Sources and where to verify

Formulas and rules above were checked against these official bodies. Check them again for your own admission year: PMDC (MDCAT), NUST (NET and merit), ICAP (CA entry), ACCA, and your provincial technical board or TEVTA (DAE). Fees and closing merits change every year and are not listed here. Use the university's own prospectus.
