About these Soil Mechanics formulas

Soil is a mixture of solids, water and air, so most soil mechanics problems begin with phase relations: the relationships between volumes and weights of the three phases. Once you can move between void ratio, porosity, water content, degree of saturation and unit weights, the later topics (permeability, consolidation, shear strength and bearing capacity) follow from them.

Unit weight of water is taken as 9.81 kN/m³ in the examples. Some courses use 10 kN/m³ for quick calculations, so check what your instructor expects.

How to use these formulas

  • Draw a phase diagram with volumes on one side and weights on the other. Mark what the question gives before choosing a formula.
  • Convert water content from a percentage to a decimal in formulas such as γd = γ / (1 + w).
  • Use effective stress, not total stress, in the shear strength and consolidation formulas.

Worked example

Question. A saturated soil has specific gravity of solids G = 2.70 and water content w = 20%. Take γw = 9.81 kN/m³.

Saturated, so S = 1 and e = wG / S = 0.20 × 2.70 = 0.54.

Porosity n = e / (1 + e) = 0.54 / 1.54 = 0.351.

Saturated unit weight = (G + e) / (1 + e) × γw = (2.70 + 0.54) / 1.54 × 9.81 = 20.64 kN/m³.

Dry unit weight = G γw / (1 + e) = 17.20 kN/m³. Check: γsat / (1 + w) = 20.64 / 1.20 = 17.20 kN/m³.

Common mistakes

  • Entering water content as 20 instead of 0.20 in γd = γ / (1 + w).
  • Mixing up saturated and submerged unit weight. The submerged (buoyant) unit weight is γsat − γw.
  • Using total stress in Coulomb's shear strength equation when the question asks about effective stress.

FAQ

What is the difference between void ratio and porosity?

Void ratio is the volume of voids divided by the volume of solids. Porosity is the volume of voids divided by the total volume. They are linked by n = e / (1 + e).

What does the overconsolidation ratio tell me?

It compares the maximum past effective pressure on a soil with its present effective overburden pressure. An OCR greater than 1 means the soil has been loaded more heavily in the past and will be less compressible.

Which Rankine coefficient is used for a retaining wall?

The active coefficient Ka is used when the wall can move slightly away from the soil. The passive coefficient Kp applies when the wall pushes against the soil.
