About these Time Value of Money formulas

Time value of money rests on one idea: a rupee today is worth more than a rupee later, because today's rupee can earn interest. Every formula on this page is a version of that idea. Lump-sum formulas (FV and PV) move a single amount through time, annuity formulas handle equal payments such as loan instalments, and perpetuity formulas handle payments that never stop.

The most common source of wrong answers is a mismatch between the rate and the period. If the rate is annual but payments are monthly, convert both before you substitute.

How to use these formulas

  • Decide first whether you have a single amount, a series of equal payments, or an endless stream. That picks the formula family.
  • Match the rate to the period. For monthly payments divide the annual rate by 12 and multiply the number of years by 12.
  • For an annuity due (payments at the start of each period), multiply the ordinary annuity result by (1 + r).

Worked example

Question. Three quick problems.

Future value: Rs. 100,000 invested at 10% a year, compounded annually for 3 years. FV = 100,000 × (1.10)³ = Rs. 133,100.

Annuity: what is the present value of Rs. 20,000 received at the end of each year for 5 years at 8%? PV = 20,000 × [1 − (1.08)⁻⁵] / 0.08 = Rs. 79,854.

Effective rate: 12% a year compounded monthly gives EAR = (1 + 0.12/12)¹² − 1 = 12.68%.

Rule of 72: at 8%, money doubles in about 72 / 8 = 9 years. The exact answer is 9.01 years, so the shortcut is close.

Common mistakes

  • Putting the rate in as 10 instead of 0.10. Only the simple interest formula above divides by 100.
  • Treating an annuity due as an ordinary annuity. Payments at the start of the period earn one extra period of interest.
  • Forgetting that PV = C / r values the first payment one period from now, not today.

FAQ

What is the difference between the nominal and the effective annual rate?

The nominal rate ignores compounding inside the year. The effective annual rate includes it. With 12% compounded monthly, the effective annual rate is about 12.68%.

When should I use the rule of 72?

As a quick mental estimate of how long money takes to double. It works best for rates between about 6% and 10%. Use the exact formula when you need accuracy.

How is a loan EMI calculated?

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ / [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1], where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of monthly instalments.
