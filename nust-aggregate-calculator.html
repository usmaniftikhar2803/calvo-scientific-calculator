The formula

NUST merit for NET-based admission: Aggregate = (Matric ÷ total × 10) + (FSc ÷ total × 15) + (NET ÷ 200 × 75). The NET carries three quarters of the merit, so it decides most of your aggregate.

Worked example

Matric 950 of 1100 at 10%: 950 ÷ 1100 × 10 = 8.64. FSc 480 of 550 at 15%: 480 ÷ 550 × 15 = 13.09. NET 140 of 200 at 75%: 140 ÷ 200 × 75 = 52.50. Aggregate = 8.64 + 13.09 + 52.50 = 74.23%.

Eligibility

Secondary sources for the 2026 cycle mention a minimum of 60% in both SSC and HSSC for eligibility. Confirm the exact criteria for your program on NUST's admissions page.

Important

NUST may adjust its merit criteria between admission cycles. This calculator gives an estimate. Weightages, and even which marks count, can change from one admission cycle to the next, so always confirm with the official prospectus or admission notice. The published merit list is final. NUST merit: why the NET dominates and how to plan around it

NUST admission looks different from the usual Pakistani merit formula. Instead of giving roughly equal importance to board marks and the entry test, it places three quarters of the total on the NUST Entry Test (NET). That has big consequences for how you prepare, how you read your chances, and how you compare yourself with other applicants. This guide explains the formula, runs worked examples and shows what actually changes your aggregate.

The formula in plain words

The calculator uses 10% Matric, 15% FSc and 75% NET, with the NET marked out of 200. Aggregate = (Matric ÷ Matric total × 10) + (FSc ÷ FSc total × 15) + (NET ÷ 200 × 75). These are the weights reported for NUST in recent cycles. NUST uses FSc Part-I if Part-II is not yet available, and it can revise the formula or the way marks are counted, so verify the details in the prospectus for your year.

Worked example 1

Matric 950 of 1100, FSc 990 of 1100, NET 150 of 200.

  • Matric: 950 ÷ 1100 × 10 = 8.64
  • FSc: 990 ÷ 1100 × 15 = 13.50
  • NET: 150 ÷ 200 × 75 = 56.25

Aggregate = 78.39%. Even with an excellent FSc, the NET contributes by far the largest share.

Worked example 2

Matric 900 of 1100, FSc Part-I 430 of 550, NET 170 of 200. The components are 8.18, 11.73 and 63.75, giving 83.66%. This student has lower board marks than the first, but a higher NET puts the aggregate more than five points ahead.

One NET mark is worth about 27 FSc marks, and a ten-mark improvement in NET is worth as much as roughly 270 extra FSc marks. In other words, nothing you can still change matters as much as the NET.

High FSc with a low NET, and the reverse

Compare two applicants with the same Matric of 1000. Applicant A has an outstanding FSc of 1050 but only 110 in NET and ends with 64.66%. Applicant B has a weaker FSc of 900 but scores 150 in NET and gets 77.61%. The second student leads by almost thirteen points. Strong board marks help, but they cannot make up for a low entry-test score at NUST.

Using the target tool

Enter your Matric and FSc marks, then type the aggregate you want. With Matric 900 and FSc 1000, 75% needs about 142 NET marks, 80% needs about 155, and 85% needs about 168. If the required NET exceeds 200, the target cannot be reached with your board marks. Use this to decide how much to aim for in each NET attempt, and which programmes to list.

Programme choice and NET variation

NUST programmes close at different levels, and the cut-offs shift with the paper's difficulty, the number of applicants, and the number of seats. A good aggregate is the minimum to be competitive for the programme you want, not a guarantee. For popular programmes such as computing and electrical engineering, the closing aggregate is usually higher than for less crowded disciplines. Check how NUST handles multiple NET series, since some cycles allow you to use your best score, and confirm it before you plan retakes.

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong total for NET. The NET is out of 200. A percentage entered as the raw score will understate your result.
  • Using the wrong FSc marks. If your Part-II result is not out, use the Part-I marks and Part-I total, such as 430 of 550.
  • Treating high FSc as a safety net. At 15%, it moves the aggregate only slightly.
  • Using the formula for other universities. NUST's weights are specific to NUST. Other universities use different splits.
  • Ignoring minimum eligibility. NUST publishes eligibility conditions such as minimum qualifying marks, so check them for your programme before applying.

What this means for preparation

Because the NET carries 75%, your best use of time is NET-style practice: timed mixed papers in Mathematics, Physics and the other tested subjects, followed by review of every mistake. Moving from 140 to 160 adds 7.5 aggregate points, more than any change in board marks could ever deliver. Keep your FSc preparation reasonable so you are not sacrificing it entirely, but allocate the majority of your remaining time to the NET.

A study plan built from the formula

Suppose your FSc and Matric are fixed and your current NET practice average is 130. To reach 150 you need 20 more marks, which the calculator shows is worth 7.5 aggregate points. Break the 20 marks into smaller targets: perhaps 8 from fewer calculation errors, 7 from topics you have not revised properly, and 5 from better time management in the last third of the paper. Track each practice paper against those three buckets. This turns a vague goal such as "score higher" into three specific habits that you can measure every week.

Checking your own numbers

Do one manual check before you trust any estimate. Write your three percentages, multiply them by 0.10, 0.15 and 0.75 and add them. For 82%, 90% and 70%, the sum is 8.20 + 13.50 + 52.50 = 74.20. If the calculator shows something different, check that the NET total is 200 and that your FSc row uses the marks that NUST will count.

FAQ

What is the NUST merit formula?

Aggregate = 10% Matric + 15% FSc + 75% NET for NET-based undergraduate admission. Convert each result to a percentage, multiply by its weightage and add them.

How many marks is the NET out of?

The NET is out of 200. Enter 200 as the total in the NET row.

Which NET score counts if I gave it more than once?

NUST considers your best NET score across attempts for merit. Enter that best score.

Do business and social sciences programs use the same weights?

Some programs use a different split. This page uses the engineering and computing split (10 / 15 / 75). Edit the weights if your program differs.

How many NET marks do I need for my target merit?

Enter your Matric and FSc marks, type your target aggregate in the second box and press the button.

Is the result my official merit?

No. It is an estimate. NUST's published merit list is final.
