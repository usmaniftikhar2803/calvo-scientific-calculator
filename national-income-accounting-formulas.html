About these National Income Accounting formulas

National income accounting measures the size of an economy in three equivalent ways: by what is spent on final goods (expenditure), by what is earned as wages, rent, interest and profit (income), and by the value added at each stage of production (output). The formulas here start with those three methods and then move from GDP to GNP, NNP and national income.

The second half covers the Keynesian consumption function and the multiplier, which show how a change in investment spending leads to a larger change in national income.

How to use these formulas

  • Identify which measure the question wants: GDP, GNP, NDP, NNP or national income. Each adds or subtracts one item from the previous one.
  • Check whether figures are at market price or factor cost. Market price = factor cost + indirect taxes − subsidies.
  • For multiplier problems, find MPC first. Then k = 1 / (1 − MPC) and ΔY = k × ΔI.

Worked example

Question. In a year, consumption C = 600, investment I = 150, government spending G = 200, exports X = 100 and imports M = 120 (all in Rs. billion). Net factor income from abroad is 40 and depreciation is 70.

GDP = C + I + G + (X − M) = 600 + 150 + 200 + (100 − 120) = 930.

GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad = 930 + 40 = 970.

NNP = GNP − depreciation = 970 − 70 = 900.

If MPC = 0.8, the multiplier k = 1 / (1 − 0.8) = 5, so an extra investment of 10 raises income by 50.

Common mistakes

  • Adding imports instead of subtracting them in the expenditure formula. Net exports are X − M.
  • Using nominal GDP to compare growth across years. Use real GDP, found by dividing by the GDP deflator.
  • Mixing market price and factor cost figures when moving from NNP to national income.

FAQ

What is the difference between GDP and GNP?

GDP counts output produced inside a country's borders, whoever owns the factors of production. GNP counts output produced by the country's own residents, wherever they are, so it adds net factor income from abroad.

Why is the multiplier 1 / (1 − MPC)?

Each round of spending creates income, and a fraction MPC of that income is spent again. Adding all the rounds gives a geometric series with sum 1 / (1 − MPC).

What is the difference between nominal and real GDP?

Nominal GDP uses current prices. Real GDP uses constant base-year prices, so it measures changes in output without the effect of inflation.
