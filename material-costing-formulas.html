About these Material Costing formulas

Material is usually the largest item in the cost of a product, so cost accounting spends a lot of time on how much to order, when to order and how to price what is issued to production. The formulas below cover the order quantity (EOQ and EPQ), the stock levels that trigger an order, and the measures used to monitor stock.

Most problems give annual demand, the cost of placing one order and the cost of holding one unit for a year. Keep all three on the same time basis before you substitute.

How to use these formulas

  • Write down D (annual demand), S (cost per order) and H (holding cost per unit per year) before choosing a formula.
  • At the EOQ, annual ordering cost equals annual holding cost. Use this as a check on your answer.
  • Set the reorder level from usage and lead time, then add safety stock if the question mentions uncertainty in demand or delivery.

Worked example

Question. Annual demand is 12,000 units, ordering cost is Rs. 300 per order and holding cost is Rs. 12 per unit per year. Daily usage is 40 units, lead time is 10 days and safety stock is 100 units.

EOQ = √(2DS/H) = √(2 × 12,000 × 300 / 12) = 774.6 units (about 775).

Orders per year = 12,000 / 774.6 = 15.5.

Annual ordering cost = 15.49 × 300 = Rs. 4,648 and annual holding cost = (774.6 / 2) × 12 = Rs. 4,648. They are equal, as expected at the EOQ.

Reorder level = (40 × 10) + 100 = 500 units.

Common mistakes

  • Using monthly demand with an annual holding cost. Put D, S and H on the same yearly basis.
  • Treating the cost per order as a total. S is the cost of placing one order, not all orders in the year.
  • Leaving out safety stock when the question mentions uncertain lead time or demand.

FAQ

What does the EOQ model assume?

Constant demand, a fixed cost per order, a fixed holding cost per unit, no shortages and immediate delivery of each order. When these do not hold, the EOQ is only a starting point.

What is the difference between EOQ and reorder level?

EOQ says how much to order. The reorder level says when to order, based on usage during the lead time plus any safety stock.

What is ABC analysis?

It ranks stock items by annual usage value (units × unit cost). A small number of items (A) usually account for most of the value and get the tightest control, while many low-value items (C) get simple control.
