The aggregate formula

Most merit lists use a weighted percentage: Aggregate = Σ (Obtained ÷ Total × Weightage), where the weightages add up to 100.

Worked example

Matric 900 of 1100 at 10%: 900 ÷ 1100 × 10 = 8.18. Intermediate 800 of 1100 at 40%: 800 ÷ 1100 × 40 = 29.09. Entry test 120 of 200 at 50%: 120 ÷ 200 × 50 = 30.00. Aggregate = 8.18 + 29.09 + 30.00 = 67.27%.

Important

Weightages, and even which marks count, change by university, program and year. Some add interview or hafiz-e-Quran marks. Always follow the official admission notice. This calculator is only for estimating. To turn a percentage into a grade, see the percentage calculator.

How merit aggregate works in Pakistan, and how to use this calculator for any university

Almost every admission in Pakistan, whether to a medical college, an engineering university or a private business school, ranks applicants by an "aggregate". The idea is simple: each part of your academic record is converted into a percentage, multiplied by a weight, and the weighted parts are added. The details are where students make mistakes. This guide explains the logic, shows how to find the right weights for your university, and walks through examples so you can use the calculator above with confidence.

The three-step method

  1. Convert each result to a percentage. Divide marks obtained by the total printed on your certificate. A Matric score of 950 out of 1100 is 86.36%.
  2. Multiply by the weight. If Matric is worth 10% of the merit, then 86.36% × 0.10 = 8.64 aggregate points.
  3. Add the parts. The sum of all weighted parts is your aggregate percentage.

The calculator does this for any number of rows. You can use the built-in presets for MDCAT, ECAT, NUST and FAST, or build your own with a custom set of weights.

Where to find the right weights

The weights are set by each university or admission authority and are published in the prospectus or the admission notice. Look for a section called "merit criteria", "selection criteria" or "aggregate formula". It will say something like "10% Matric, 40% Intermediate, 50% entry test". Write down three things from the notice: the weight of each part, the total marks of each part, and which marks count. If the notice says "FSc Part-I" or "HSSC Part-I and II", use exactly that.

One profile, four universities

Take a student with Matric 900 of 1100, FSc 950 of 1100, and test percentage of 70% in each test. Under the four formulas the aggregates are:

  • MDCAT-style 10/40/50: 8.18 + 34.55 + 35.00 = 77.73%
  • FAST computing 10/40/50: the same, 77.73%
  • UET-style 17/50/33: 13.91 + 43.18 + 23.10 = 80.19% (assuming the same FSc percentage is used for Part-I)
  • NUST 10/15/75: 8.18 + 12.95 + 52.50 = 73.63%

The same student is more than six points higher under the UET formula than under NUST. A student with strong boards and a moderate test should look at universities where boards carry more weight, and a student with weak boards but a strong test should do the opposite.

Using the custom option

Many private universities and some public ones publish their own weights, for example 25% Matric, 25% FSc and 50% test. Choose "Custom" in the formula list, change the row names, totals and weights, and add a row for any extra component, such as interview marks or a portfolio. The calculator checks that your obtained marks do not exceed the total, but it does not force the weights to add up to 100, so do that check yourself. A bonus box is available for marks added directly to the aggregate, such as a documented category bonus allowed by the admission notice.

Reverse calculation: what test score do I need?

Fill in all rows except the entry test, enter the aggregate you want and press the button. The tool subtracts what your Matric and FSc already contribute and divides the remainder by the test weight. For example, with 25/25/50 weights, Matric 900 of 1100 and FSc 900 of 1100, the academic parts give 40.91 points. To reach 70% you need 29.09 more points from the test, which is 58.2% of the test marks. This is the quickest way to turn a vague goal into a specific score to aim for.

O/A levels, diplomas and foreign qualifications

If you studied O/A levels or a foreign qualification, most universities ask for an equivalence certificate from the Inter Board Committee of Chairmen (IBCC). The equivalent marks on that certificate are what get used in the aggregate, not your Cambridge grades. Apply for it early, because the process takes time, and use the marks and total printed on the certificate in the calculator.

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong total. Not every certificate is out of 1100. Some are 1050, 900 or another number, so type what your document shows.
  • Using the wrong part of the result. A formula may need FSc Part-I only, full HSSC, or provisional marks. Use what the notice says.
  • Entering the test marks as a percentage in a row with a different total. If your total is 200 and you enter 70, the tool reads it as 35%.
  • Using last year's weights. Weights can change from one cycle to another, so check the current notice.
  • Expecting the estimate to equal the official list. The official list may apply rounding, category adjustments or provisional marks.

Beyond the aggregate

The aggregate is only one factor. Admission also depends on seats, quotas, domicile, minimum eligibility marks and the number of applicants. Treat your aggregate as a way to compare yourself with last year's merit lists and to decide how many backups to apply for. Then keep checking the official notice, since final merit lists, dates and requirements are decided there.

This calculator provides estimates. Always confirm weights, totals and eligibility in the official prospectus or admission notice for your year.

A habit that saves mistakes

Before you apply, calculate your aggregate under every formula that matters to you and save the results in one note. Include the date, the source of each weight and the totals you used. When the official merit list appears, you can compare your own number with the published one and spot an error in either of them straight away. If they differ by more than a point, the usual causes are a wrong total, a different set of counted marks, or a bonus you forgot to add.

FAQ

How do I calculate my merit aggregate?

Multiply each part's percentage (obtained ÷ total) by its weightage and add them up. Weightages should total 100.

Which weightage should I use?

Use the one printed in your university's admission notice or prospectus. They differ between universities and programs.

Can I add more rows, like an interview?

Yes. Press Add row and enter its marks and weightage.

Is the result my official merit?

No. It is an estimate. The university's published merit list is final.

Which formula does MDCAT use?

MDCAT uses 10% Matric + 40% FSc + 50% MDCAT (out of 180).

Which formula does UET Lahore use for ECAT?

The Fall 2026 split is 17% Matric + 50% FSc Part-I + 33% ECAT (out of 400).

How many entry test marks do I need for my target merit?

Fill in your other marks, type your target aggregate in the second box and press the button.
