Marks to Percentage Calculator

Add your subjects with obtained and total marks. Your overall percentage updates as you type, and your entries are remembered on this device.

0.00%

Formula

Percentage = (Total obtained marks ÷ Total marks) × 100. Marks of all subjects are added first, then divided by the combined total.

Example

Mathematics 50/100, Computer 69/100, Urdu 71/100: obtained = 190, total = 300, percentage = 190 ÷ 300 × 100 = 63.33%.

How the calculation works

This calculator adds up every mark you obtained and every mark that was available, then divides the first number by the second. It does not average the individual subject percentages. The two methods give the same answer only when every subject has the same total. When totals differ, for example a 50-mark paper next to a 100-mark paper, the combined method is the correct one, and it is the one used for a result card.

Example with different totals

Physics 38/50, Chemistry 70/75 and Mathematics 92/100. Obtained = 38 + 70 + 92 = 200. Total = 50 + 75 + 100 = 225. Percentage = 200 ÷ 225 × 100 = 88.89%.

If you averaged the three subject percentages (76%, 93.33% and 92%) you would get 87.11%. That number does not match how a combined total is calculated, which is why this tool always works with total marks.

Theory and practical marks

You can enter a subject as one row or split it into two. Chemistry theory 52/65 and practical 30/35 can be added as two rows, or as one row of 82/100. The final percentage is identical, because the obtained marks and the total marks are simply summed.

Matric and intermediate results

Many boards give a total of 1100 for matric (9th class 550 plus 10th class 550) and for FSc (part 1 plus part 2). Check your own result card for the exact totals. To get a combined percentage, add two rows: 9th class 470/550 and 10th class 465/550. The total is 935/1100, which is 85.00%.

A quick mental shortcut: when the total is 600, divide your marks by 6; for 500 divide by 5; for 1000 divide by 10; for 1100 divide by 11. For example, 468 out of 600 is 468 ÷ 6 = 78%.

How many marks do you still need?

Suppose you have 190 out of 300 and one 100-mark paper is left. You want 65% overall. The overall total will be 400, so you need 65% of 400 = 260 marks. You already have 190, so you need 70 marks in the last paper. The method is: target percentage × final total ÷ 100, minus the marks you already have.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by the number of subjects instead of the total marks.
  • Entering a percentage in the marks box. Enter the raw marks you got.
  • Leaving the total at 100 for a paper that is out of 50 or 75.
  • Forgetting a subject, or adding an optional subject that the board does not count.

Your entries stay on your device

The subjects you type are saved in your browser, so they are still there when you come back. Press Save to History to keep a finished result in the Calvo History page. Clearing the site data of your browser removes them, and nothing is sent to a server.

FAQ

Can I use this for university results?

Yes, for a simple percentage of marks. Most universities report a GPA or CGPA based on credit hours and grade points, so for that use the GPA calculator or the CGPA calculator.

Why is my result different from my board result?

Usually because of rounding, an optional subject that the board leaves out, or a different total. Boards may also round to a whole number. Compare the obtained and total marks shown under the result with your result card.

Do I have to type subject names?

No. The subject name is optional and only helps you recognise each row.

Can I enter decimal marks?

Yes. Marks such as 45.5 out of 50 work fine.

How do I convert my CGPA to a percentage?

That needs a different formula. Use the CGPA to Percentage calculator.
