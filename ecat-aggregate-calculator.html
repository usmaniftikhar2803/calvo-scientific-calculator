The formula

UET Lahore merit for ECAT-based programs: Aggregate = (Matric ÷ total × 17) + (FSc Part-I ÷ Part-I total × 50) + (ECAT ÷ 400 × 33). UET states that merit is calculated on FSc Part-I (first year) marks together with the ECAT score.

Worked example

Matric 950 of 1100 at 17%: 950 ÷ 1100 × 17 = 14.68. FSc Part-I 480 of 550 at 50%: 480 ÷ 550 × 50 = 43.64. ECAT 260 of 400 at 33%: 260 ÷ 400 × 33 = 21.45. Aggregate = 14.68 + 43.64 + 21.45 = 79.77%.

About the ECAT score

According to UET's admission FAQ, the ECAT is out of 400 with 4 marks for each correct answer and 0 for an unanswered question. There is no minimum passing score, but you need at least 60% in Intermediate to apply for engineering programs and an overall aggregate of at least 50%.

Why the weights may differ

UET has used different splits in different sessions (for example 25% / 45% / 30% in earlier years), and some non-ECAT programs have their own formula. This page uses the 17 / 50 / 33 split listed for Fall 2026. If your program or session uses different weights, edit them in the calculator.

Important

Weightages for UET Lahore can change every session. This calculator gives an estimate. Weightages, and even which marks count, can change from one admission cycle to the next, so always confirm with the official prospectus or admission notice. The published merit list is final. ECAT and UET merit: how the aggregate is built and how to improve it

Engineering admission in Punjab combines your academic record with the ECAT, but not in the same way as medical admission. At UET Lahore the largest share goes to your FSc Part-I, not to the entry test. That changes how you should read your score, plan your remaining preparation and judge whether a programme is within reach. This section explains the formula, shows worked examples and points out the details that most often cause a wrong estimate.

The formula used by the calculator

The calculator uses 17% Matric, 50% FSc Part-I and 33% ECAT, with ECAT marked out of 400. Aggregate = (Matric ÷ Matric total × 17) + (FSc Part-I ÷ Part-I total × 50) + (ECAT ÷ 400 × 33). These are the weightages reported for UET Lahore in recent cycles, but UET has used different splits across intakes, and other engineering universities may use other weights or other tests, so confirm the formula for your session on the official admission portal. You can edit the weights in any row.

Worked example 1

Matric 950 of 1100, FSc Part-I 480 of 550, ECAT 300 of 400.

  • Matric: 950 ÷ 1100 × 17 = 14.68
  • FSc Part-I: 480 ÷ 550 × 50 = 43.64
  • ECAT: 300 ÷ 400 × 33 = 24.75

Aggregate = 83.07%. Almost half of the total comes from FSc Part-I, and only 24.75 points come from the test, even though the student scored 75% in it.

Worked example 2

Matric 900 of 1100, FSc Part-I 430 of 550, ECAT 260 of 400. The parts are 13.91, 39.09 and 21.45, giving an aggregate of 74.45%. Compared with example 1, the student is 50 marks lower in Matric, 50 lower in Part-I and 40 lower in ECAT, and the aggregate is nearly nine points lower.

A single ECAT mark and a single Part-I mark are worth almost the same. The difference is that you can still improve the ECAT, while Part-I is fixed once the result card is issued. When you plan how many marks to chase, divide your gap by 0.083.

Part-I or full FSc?

The tool's default uses Part-I because UET Lahore's merit has been reported to count first-year marks. Many students mistakenly enter full FSc marks out of 1100. The result can be badly off: 480 out of 550 is 87.3%, which gives 43.64 points, but entering 480 out of 1100 gives only 21.82. Equally, if you enter 960 out of 1100, you are using different data from what the merit list used. If your university counts Part-II or both parts, change the label and totals in the row before calculating.

Who needs the ECAT?

In Punjab, the entry test is normally a requirement for admission to engineering programmes at public universities and many affiliated institutions. Other provinces and universities use their own tests, such as NET for NUST or the NU test for FAST, and some use ETEA or other bodies. A score from one test does not usually transfer to another university's merit, so check which test the university accepts before registering.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong Part-I total. Part-I is usually out of 550, but your result card is the only reliable source.
  • Entering the ECAT out of 100. The total is 400, so a raw score of 78 is 19.5%, not 78%.
  • Using one session's weights for another. Weightages can change between years or between campuses.
  • Mixing FSc streams. Pre-engineering, ICS and other subject groups may have different totals or eligibility rules.
  • Ignoring minimum eligibility. Programmes often require a minimum percentage in FSc, such as 60% for engineering, so check the criteria for your programme as well as your aggregate.

Using the calculator for a realistic plan

Start with your Matric and Part-I marks and enter a target aggregate of the previous year's closing merit for your preferred programme. The tool will show how many ECAT marks you need. If the answer is above about 350, the target is probably unrealistic and you should list backup programmes. If it is between 250 and 300, focus on accuracy in Mathematics and Physics, which usually carry the most questions.

This is an estimate. UET and other universities publish the official formula in their admission notice, and the published merit list is final.

Choosing programmes with the aggregate in mind

Different engineering programmes close at different merit levels, and the order changes from year to year. Computer-related and electrical programmes tend to attract the strongest applicants, while some other disciplines close lower. Instead of relying on one dream programme, build a list of three groups: a reach programme where your aggregate is a few points below last year's closing merit, a realistic programme where you are close to it, and a safe programme where you are comfortably above it. Use the calculator to see which group each programme belongs to after you enter your own marks, and revisit the list when the new merit lists appear.

A quick way to double-check the result by hand

Convert each of your three results to a percentage and apply the weights with a basic calculator: Matric% × 0.17 + Part-I% × 0.50 + ECAT% × 0.33. For example, 86.4%, 78.2% and 65% give 14.69 + 39.10 + 21.45 = 75.24. If this matches the tool's answer, your inputs are consistent. If the two differ, the most likely cause is a wrong total in one of the three rows.

FAQ

What is the UET Lahore aggregate formula?

For ECAT-based programs in Fall 2026 the split is 17% Matric, 50% FSc Part-I and 33% ECAT. Convert each to a percentage, multiply by its weightage and add them.

What is the ECAT out of?

The ECAT is out of 400 marks. Enter 400 as the total in the ECAT row.

Does UET use FSc Part-I or the full FSc?

UET's FAQ says merit is based on First Year / Part-I marks together with the ECAT score. Enter your Part-I obtained marks and Part-I total.

Is there negative marking in ECAT?

UET's FAQ marks 4 for a correct answer and 0 for no answer, and its example gives no deduction for wrong answers. Check the latest ECAT notice to be sure.

How many ECAT marks do I need for my target merit?

Enter your Matric and FSc Part-I marks, type your target aggregate in the second box and press the button.

Is the result my official merit?

No. It is an estimate. UET's published merit list is final.
