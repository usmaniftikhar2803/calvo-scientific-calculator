Unit Converter

By Hafiz Muhammad Usman · Last updated 3 November 2026 · 15 categories including currency

The unit converter handles the conversions students and everyday users need most. Pick a category, type a value, choose the units on each side and the answer appears straight away. You can swap the two sides with one tap.

There are 15 categories: length, weight, temperature, area, volume, speed, time, data, pressure, energy, power, force, angle, frequency and currency. Temperature is handled separately because it is not a simple multiplication, and currency uses exchange rates rather than fixed ratios.

What it can do

  • Physics-friendly units: pressure, energy, power, force, angle and frequency are included, which saves a trip to the textbook table in numerical problems.
  • Temperature done correctly: Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin use the proper offset formulas, not a single factor.
  • Currency: rates come from ExchangeRate-API when you are online, and the last rates that were saved on your device are used if you are offline. Rates are for guidance, so check your bank for the exact rate you will get.
  • Swap in one tap: a swap button reverses the two units, so converting back takes no retyping.

How to use it

  1. Open the converter and choose a category such as Length or Currency.
  2. Type the value and pick the unit you are converting from.
  3. Pick the unit you want. The result updates as you type.
  4. Use the swap button to convert the other way.

Two conversions to check by hand

  • 5 km to miles: 5 × 0.621371 ≈ 3.107 miles.
  • 100 °F to °C: (100 − 32) × 5 ÷ 9 ≈ 37.78 °C.

Tips for better results

  • In physics numericals, convert everything to SI units (metres, kilograms, seconds) before using a formula.
  • Currency rates change during the day. Treat the result as an estimate.

The factor-label method

Every conversion is multiplication by a fraction equal to 1. To change 72 km/h into m/s, multiply by 1000 m/1 km and by 1 h/3600 s: 72 × 1000 ÷ 3600 = 20 m/s. Writing the units in the fraction lets you cancel them and confirm you have the right units left, which catches most mistakes before the arithmetic does.

Conversions that surprise people

  • Area and volume: 1 m² is 10,000 cm², not 100, because both sides are squared. 1 litre is 1,000 cm³.
  • Temperature: °F = °C × 9/5 + 32, so 37 °C = 98.6 °F. Kelvin = °C + 273.15. Differences in temperature use no offset, since a 10 °C rise is a 10 K rise.
  • Data sizes: storage makers use decimal units (1 GB = 1,000,000,000 bytes) while many systems show binary units (1 GiB = 2³⁰ bytes). A “500 GB” drive therefore appears as about 465 GiB: 500 × 10⁹ ÷ 2³⁰ ≈ 465.7.
  • Pressure: 1 atm = 101,325 Pa = 760 mmHg.
  • Energy: 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ, and 1 calorie = 4.184 J. A “Calorie” on food labels is a kilocalorie.
  • Length: 1 inch = 2.54 cm exactly, and 1 mile = 1.609344 km.

Currency: guidance only

Currency is different from physical units because the rate changes. The converter shows a rate for guidance, and the rate a bank, exchange house or card network gives you will include its own margin and fees. For an actual payment, check the quoted rate and total cost before you commit.

Check the size of your answer

After converting, ask whether the number is sensible. A walking speed of 1.4 m/s is about 5 km/h, a car at 100 km/h is about 28 m/s, and normal body temperature is about 37 °C. If your result is wildly different, look for a missed power of ten.

Frequently asked questions

Which currencies are supported?

A long list of world currencies, including PKR, USD, EUR, GBP, SAR and AED, depending on what the rate source provides.

Is the currency rate live?

It is fetched when you are online and refreshed regularly. Offline, the last saved rate is used, so it may be out of date.

Does it need an internet connection?

Only currency needs one for fresh rates. All other categories use fixed conversion factors and work offline.

Why does my 1 TB drive show less space?

The maker counts 1 TB as 10¹² bytes, while the operating system may divide by 2⁴⁰ and show about 0.91 TiB. Some space is also used by formatting.

Is a temperature difference converted the same way as a temperature?

No. A difference of 10 °C equals a difference of 10 K and 18 °F. The +32 and +273.15 offsets apply only to actual temperatures.
